6.1 Ticker-List Rationale
The original Financial Services ticker list did not fully represent several major banking and capital markets institutions. To improve industry coverage, we expanded the list by adding additional large financial firms, including Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, Citibank, Wells Fargo, and Discover. These additions allowed the dataset to capture a broader range of operational, regulatory, and expansion-related events occurring across the financial sector. The seeded list also tended to overrepresent a smaller subset of financial firms, limiting the diversity of filings returned during event extraction.
For the Travel and Hospitality industry, the seeded ticker list underrepresented large resort operators, casinos, and entertainment-based hospitality companies. To address this limitation, we added companies associated with resorts and destination entertainment properties in order to increase the variety of filings collected. These modifications improved event diversity and helped capture operational activities beyond traditional hotel chains, including acquisitions, property developments, and large-scale hospitality expansions.

6.2 Search-Phrase Rationale
We expanded the original phrase list by adding terms associated with operational growth, restructuring, and organizational change. Additional phrases such as “new branch,” “branch closure,” “operations center,” and “new headquarters” improved the identification of filings related to company expansion, consolidation, and strategic business development within the Financial Services industry. These phrases increased coverage of location-based operational events that were not consistently captured by the seeded phrase list.
Within the Travel and Hospitality category, the original search phrases focused heavily on expansion-related activity and did not adequately capture indicators of declining performance, restructuring, or asset transfers. To improve coverage, we added phrases including “new acquisition,” “completed the acquisition,” “sold its,” and “sale of the hotel.” These additions strengthened the detection of mergers, acquisitions, property sales, and restructuring activities commonly reported in hospitality-related filings. The revised phrase list also reduced the likelihood of overlooking major operational transitions within the industry.

6.3 Window-Experiment Results
We experimented with multiple filing windows ranging from 30 to 360 days in order to identify a configuration that produced a sufficient number of usable events while remaining computationally efficient. Early experiments returned relatively few valid events because some filings contained incomplete or inconsistent ticker metadata. To improve event retrieval, we adjusted the filtering logic to better handle filings with missing ticker information and reduce unnecessary exclusions.
To remain within the project’s $3.00 API cost ceiling, the maximum candidate count was capped at 50. Increasing either the filing window size or candidate count significantly increased runtime and API usage without consistently improving event quality. After evaluating several configurations, the 30-day filing window was selected because both industries exceeded the minimum target of eight geocoded events while maintaining relatively low processing costs and manageable runtime requirements.

6.4 Stage 3 Classification Quality per Industry
The Stage 3 classification process generally produced accurate event labels across both industries. Most classified events were identified as “opening” events, while smaller numbers were categorized as “closing,” “expansion,” and “acquisition” events. In total, 11 geocoded events were classified as openings, while one event each was labeled as a closing, expansion, and acquisition.
Most false positives occurred when filings referenced operational or organizational changes using broad or ambiguous language that did not clearly correspond to a location-based event. Despite these limitations, the classification model performed effectively overall because many filings used consistent terminology associated with business openings, closures, acquisitions, and expansion activities. The use of refined search phrases and industry-specific ticker lists also improved classification precision across both datasets.

6.5 Limitations
One major limitation of the project was the restriction on candidate count and API spending. To remain below the $3.00 cost ceiling, the candidate count was capped at 50, which reduced the total number of filings and events available for analysis. This limitation likely affected the completeness of the geographic trends displayed in the Folium maps and reduced the number of events available for comparative analysis across industries.
Additionally, shorter filing windows and smaller candidate pools may have excluded relevant events occurring outside the selected timeframe. Although expanding the filing window or increasing the candidate count would likely improve event coverage and provide a more comprehensive representation of activity within the Financial Services and Travel and Hospitality industries, doing so would also substantially increase computational requirements and API costs.
